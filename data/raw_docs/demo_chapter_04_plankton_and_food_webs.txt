Chapter 4 Plankton and Marine Food Webs

Plankton are organisms that drift with currents. Phytoplankton are photosynthetic and form the base of many marine food webs. They use sunlight, carbon dioxide, and dissolved nutrients to produce organic matter. Zooplankton feed on phytoplankton, bacteria, detritus, and other zooplankton.

Marine food webs transfer energy from primary producers to consumers. Small fishes, jellyfish, and filter feeders consume plankton. Larger fishes, seabirds, marine mammals, and sharks feed at higher trophic levels. Energy is lost at each transfer, so food webs depend on sustained primary production.

Phytoplankton growth is often limited by nutrients such as nitrate, phosphate, iron, or silica. Upwelling zones, estuaries, and polar seasonal blooms can support high productivity because nutrients are available near sunlit surface waters. In contrast, clear tropical ocean waters may have low nutrient availability despite abundant light.

Plankton communities also influence global biogeochemical cycles. Through the biological pump, sinking particles carry carbon from surface waters into the deep ocean. This process links microscopic organisms to climate regulation.
