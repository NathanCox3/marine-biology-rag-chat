Chapter 8 Pollution and Eutrophication

Marine pollution includes plastics, oil, heavy metals, pesticides, sewage, nutrients, noise, and chemical contaminants. Pollution can harm organisms directly through toxicity or indirectly by changing habitat quality and food webs.

Nutrient pollution from agriculture, wastewater, and urban runoff can cause eutrophication. Excess nitrogen and phosphorus stimulate algal blooms. When algae die and decompose, microbial respiration can deplete dissolved oxygen, creating hypoxic zones that stress or kill marine life.

Plastic pollution affects marine organisms through ingestion, entanglement, and habitat alteration. Microplastics can be eaten by plankton, shellfish, fishes, and seabirds. The ecological effects depend on particle size, concentration, chemical additives, and exposure pathways.

Oil spills can coat feathers and fur, reduce insulation, poison organisms, and damage coastal habitats. Cleanup is difficult because oil can spread, sink, emulsify, or persist in sediments. Prevention and rapid response are central to reducing damage.
