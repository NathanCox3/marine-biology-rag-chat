Chapter 7 Fisheries and Conservation

Fisheries provide food and livelihoods but can reduce fish populations when harvest exceeds replacement. Overfishing occurs when removals are too high for a population to sustain. It can change age structure, reduce reproductive output, and alter food-web interactions.

Bycatch is the unintended capture of non-target species such as seabirds, turtles, sharks, and juvenile fishes. Gear modifications, seasonal closures, spatial closures, and improved monitoring can reduce bycatch. Habitat damage can also occur when fishing gear contacts sensitive seafloor communities.

Marine protected areas can conserve biodiversity and support fisheries when they protect important habitats, spawning grounds, or nursery areas. Effective protection depends on enforcement, ecological design, local support, and connectivity among habitats.

Sustainable fisheries management uses catch limits, size limits, gear restrictions, stock assessments, and ecosystem-based approaches. Conservation also benefits from reducing pollution, protecting coastal habitats, and addressing climate stressors.
