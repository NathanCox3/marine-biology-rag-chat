Chapter 5 Ocean Zones and Habitats

Marine habitats vary with depth, distance from shore, light, temperature, salinity, oxygen, and seafloor type. The intertidal zone lies between high and low tide and exposes organisms to waves, drying, temperature shifts, and predation. Rocky shores, sandy beaches, and mudflats each support different communities.

The neritic zone covers continental shelves and often has high productivity because light reaches shallow waters and nutrients arrive from land or upwelling. The open ocean, or pelagic zone, includes vast waters beyond the shelf. Organisms there may migrate vertically to feed at night and avoid predators during the day.

The deep sea includes the bathyal, abyssal, and hadal zones. Light is absent below the photic zone, temperatures are low, pressure is high, and food may be scarce. Deep-sea organisms often rely on sinking organic matter, predation, scavenging, or chemosynthetic production near hydrothermal vents and cold seeps.

Benthic habitats occur on or within the seafloor. Sediment grain size, oxygen penetration, and organic matter supply shape benthic communities. Many benthic animals burrow, filter feed, graze, or deposit feed.
