Chapter 9 Climate Change and Ocean Acidification

Climate change affects marine ecosystems through warming, sea-level rise, oxygen loss, changing currents, stronger marine heatwaves, and altered species distributions. Many organisms shift poleward or into deeper water as temperatures change. Species that cannot move or adapt quickly may decline.

Ocean acidification occurs when seawater absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Dissolved carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid, which lowers pH and reduces carbonate ion availability. Lower carbonate availability can make it harder for corals, mollusks, some plankton, and other calcifying organisms to build shells or skeletons.

Warming and acidification can interact with local stressors such as pollution, overfishing, and habitat loss. Coral reefs are especially vulnerable because heat stress can cause bleaching and acidification can reduce calcification. Loss of reef structure reduces shelter and nursery habitat for many species.

Adaptation and mitigation strategies include reducing greenhouse gas emissions, protecting resilient habitats, restoring coastal ecosystems, improving fisheries management, and reducing local stressors that weaken ecosystem recovery.
