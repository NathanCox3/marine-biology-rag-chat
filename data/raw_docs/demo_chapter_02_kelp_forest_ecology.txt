Chapter 2 Kelp Forest Ecology

Kelp forests are temperate coastal ecosystems dominated by large brown algae. Giant kelp and related species attach to rocky seafloors with holdfasts, extend upward through the water column, and form floating canopies near the surface. This vertical structure creates habitat for organisms living near the bottom, in midwater, and within the canopy.

Kelp forests support fishes, sea urchins, sea stars, crabs, snails, bryozoans, and marine mammals. Juvenile fishes often use kelp blades and stipes as cover. Detritus from kelp also fuels food webs beyond the forest because broken kelp fragments drift into adjacent sandy habitats and deep water.

Grazing pressure strongly affects kelp abundance. Sea urchins consume kelp tissue and can create urchin barrens when predators are absent or reduced. Predators such as sea otters, lobsters, and some fishes can indirectly support kelp by limiting grazers. This trophic cascade is a classic example of how predator loss can alter marine habitat.

Kelp productivity depends on light, nutrients, temperature, and wave exposure. Upwelling often supplies nitrate-rich water that promotes kelp growth. Warm, nutrient-poor periods can reduce kelp growth and make forests more vulnerable to grazing and storms.
