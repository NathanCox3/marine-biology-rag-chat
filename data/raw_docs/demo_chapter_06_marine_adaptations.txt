Chapter 6 Marine Adaptations

Marine organisms show adaptations to salt water, pressure, temperature, movement, and predation. Osmoregulation helps animals control internal salt and water balance. Marine bony fishes tend to lose water to their salty surroundings and compensate by drinking seawater and excreting salts through gills and kidneys.

Buoyancy adaptations reduce the energetic cost of staying in the water column. Many fishes use swim bladders, lipid-rich tissues, or body shapes that generate lift. Plankton may have spines, flattened bodies, oil droplets, or small size to slow sinking.

Camouflage, schooling, shells, spines, toxins, speed, and behavioral changes can reduce predation risk. Countershading, where the dorsal side is darker than the ventral side, helps pelagic animals blend with the dark depths from above and bright surface from below.

Deep-sea animals face darkness, high pressure, and low food supply. Some use bioluminescence for communication, prey attraction, camouflage, or predator avoidance. Slow growth and low metabolic rates are common where food is limited.
